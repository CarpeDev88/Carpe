# CARPE outcome measurement

CARPE is successful when a person's own goals are easier to act on. Product evaluation must not reward more time, return visits, streaks, or notifications inside CARPE.

## In-app measures

- A user-authored goal and an optional weekly target.
- A voluntary check-in when the user takes a step.
- Optional feedback on whether a CARPE suggestion was helpful.
- For usage insight, the user's own assessment of whether a period of app use matched their intention.

These measures stay on the device. CARPE should show the reporting window and let the user clear the records. Missing check-ins mean only that no check-in was recorded; they do not mean failure.

## Small private alpha review

At the end of a week, invite the tester to answer only if they want:

1. Did CARPE help you take a step toward a goal you chose?
2. Did its attention observations feel accurate and respectful?
3. Did CARPE ever make you feel pressured, judged, or watched?
4. What should CARPE do differently next time?

Keep answers in the user's own control. Do not collect them automatically or require them to keep using CARPE.

## Product decisions

Use reports to identify where suggestions were useful, wrong, or unwelcome. Do not rank users, diagnose addiction, optimize for a target number of check-ins, or treat reduced screen time as universally positive. The app should remain useful when users decline every optional permission and cloud AI.
